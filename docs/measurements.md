# Measurement register

The single home for every **corpus-derived number** quoted on this project, and for its **standing**.

A number measured against the nine real OKM exports is admissible as support for a decision only if it can say what produced it. A number that cannot is not thereby wrong — it is **untraceable**, and a traceable-sounding figure that nobody can reproduce is worse than an absent one, because it gets quoted. This register exists so that a figure's standing is written down **once**, where a later correction can reach it.

That last clause is the whole point, and it is not hypothetical. GitHub issue **comments are immutable**, so every withdrawal this project has made until now landed as a *later comment that the withdrawn text cannot see*. The withdrawn `51 / 4 / 11 / 6` was still unmarked live prose in four places, including the hazard table in the resolution of [The order-dependence inventory and its tie rules](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/15). This file is editable. That is why it is here and not there.

**"Once" is a rule about rows, not only about files, and it was broken until [#60](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/60) found it.** A figure has **exactly one row**. The sections below are organised by originating ticket, so a section records a figure's **provenance and is not its location**: when a later ticket re-measures a figure, it **amends that row** rather than adding a second one. Two rows for one figure is not redundancy — it is two homes, and they drift apart silently, because each is correct on the day it is written and only one of them is ever read. The cost was concrete: the component-count multisets were recorded twice, and the copies had come to disagree about whether stream position was carried.

## The rule

A corpus-derived number used as evidence must carry the settings that produced it. It is quoted from this register, by entry, never restated at the point of use.

Seven axes may bear on a figure. State the ones that apply; a figure needing none of them is exempt.

| Axis | Pinned by |
| --- | --- |
| lattice / padding state | the export itself |
| background window, shape, support rule, combination | **`background-model-v1`** — [The background model](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/39) |
| quantile convention | **`quantile-v1`** (H&F type 2, pinned by both stated criteria) — [The quantile convention](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/36) |
| scale estimator, its calibration constant, the disagreement normalisation and the tolerance | **`scale-agreement-v1`** — [Which disagreement quantity is of record](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/46), constants from [Which two robust-scale estimators](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/31) and [Which independent oracles](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/34) |
| connectivity | fixed convention, 4 or 8, named per figure |
| level / threshold | named where the figure is a count or a thresholded set |
| instrument — kind, magnitude, reference quantity, seed, trial count, **stream position** | all six, or not a stability figure |

**Three of the seven are now pinned**, so a sweep run today has only **connectivity, level and the instrument** free.

**The six here are a figure's requirement, not the record's shape.** A stability record carries those same six and is **closed at them** ([#54](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/54)) — it previously carried a seventh field, `method`, which nothing defined, valued or read. A full instrument specification carries **nine**, because three further axes describe the instrument rather than the scan. The record was **never closed** until now, which is how it drifted from this table unnoticed: nothing compared the two lists. A figure must carry all six here because it is a *figure*; a record carries six because a *scan* has six things to say about its own perturbation.

**Figures are grandfathered by naming.** A figure names the instrument version it was taken under, and that is what carries it: figures taken under a superseded version **keep their standing**, while the new version opens empty. A protocol-definition bump therefore never downgrades a figure that accurately records what produced it.

### Standing

Every entry carries exactly one. The vocabulary is **closed**.

**Five definitions changed in one pass, by [#59](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/59)**, and every one of them closed a gap where the four were not exhaustive: `recorded` and `swept` did not exclude each other, `unpinned` was narrower than [#47](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/47)'s own ruling on it, and `unrecoverable`'s criterion was narrower than its gloss. The old and new texts are set side by side in that ticket's resolution.

| Standing | Means | May be quoted as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `recorded` | **every applicable axis is carried at a single value**, and **no variation was carried out, declared or not** — except a **census row**: a declared sweep exists, every applicable axis is carried, and the row reports **no value of the swept quantity for any member of it**, so its unit is the sweep rather than a member of it | a **point value** |
| `swept` | a **declared variation** was carried out, with its **axis named** and its **extent** declared, plus a **measured spread across it** — whatever that spread's size, **including a spread of zero** | that variation's **interval** — never as a point |
| `unpinned` | **not every applicable axis is carried.** The **negation of the carriage predicate** `recorded` and `swept` share, so a row missing one axis falls under exactly one standing and cannot sit between them | **not evidence.** History only. |
| `unrecoverable` | **not reachable in a declared sweep** — either **searched and absent**, or **unmatchable because the target was never specified** — **or retired by a stated condition.** Two disjuncts, and each row states which one holds | **retired.** Supports nothing. |

**Three sentences the four definitions depend on, stated once here.**

**A `declared sweep`** exists when **its axes and its extent are both read from the row's own settings cell**. The extent is the number of settings; the axes are the seven above. A figure cell is not a declaration: a sweep described only in prose is not declared, which is why two rows below carry the correction that moves their extent into the cell.

**A `declared variation`** is any variation a figure's settings cell declares and records — along a setting axis, or along an axis of the instrument, **which is one of the seven**. `residual-perturbation-v1` declares the `draw` axis and `payload-perturbation-v1` inherits it in full, so **the continuous draw and the `lattice-preserving` draw are one instrument at two settings of one axis, not two instruments.** A row states which axis its spread runs over, because a spread over draws is instrument variability at fixed settings and a spread over a setting axis is sensitivity to that setting, and the two do not read alike.

**The exception clause, opened and closed.** An exception was declared by [#59](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/59) for rows that satisfied none of the four: a variation was carried out, so they are not `recorded`, and no spread across it was reported or recoverable, so they are not `swept`. It named [#63](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/63) as the only way out, and **a second exception is not an allowed outcome**. That ticket has run, and the exception is **closed**: every row that stood under it is re-stood below under one of the four, and **no definition was amended to achieve it**.

**Two counts in this file were wrong while the clause was open, and both are corrected here.** The clause read **"Four rows"**, and so did nothing else: **three** rows ever carried the standing, and the chartering ticket named three. The clause therefore disagreed with its own rows *and* with the ticket it pointed at, in the same direction, which is what a count written from a plan rather than from the table looks like. **The register's index is not exempt from the rule it states** — the same clause that made this file the single home for a figure's standing could not count its own exceptions.

**What the ticket found on the way, which is why the closure is not a formality.** **The reduction the rows were reduced to was not lost — it was a minimum, and it reproduces exactly.** The three rows reported one extremum each where the standing wants a spread, and the ticket's charter assumed the extremum was unrecoverable without a run. Reading the producing script showed the reduction was `min` across the twenty trials, and a re-run reproduced all three recorded figures exactly. So nothing was superseded, and **the rows' defect was never the missing run**: it was that a minimum across a declared variation was standing in for the variation. A minimum is a point, and `swept` says a spread may be quoted as an interval and **never as a point**. Every one of the three now carries its per-export spread, with the minimum surviving as the low end of the range it belongs to.

`legacy-sweep` is the **provenance label**, not a standing, and [#59](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/59) gave it a criterion it never had: **a declared sweep exists, the figure sits in it, and no per-setting record was kept.** A `legacy-sweep` figure becomes `swept` once a spread across the sweep is supplied and becomes `unrecoverable` when a sweep settles it; **its standing does not move when the label is applied**, per [#56](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/56)'s rule that a provenance label says what was available and a standing says what a figure may be quoted as. **"Legacy" is not part of the criterion** — a sweep predating this record is not required, and the rows carrying the label happen to be legacy-era sweeps, which is why the mismatch between the name and the criterion costs nothing today. **The name is not renamed here.** The rows whose figures were taken under an **undeclared** protocol are **not** `legacy-sweep`, and the label's own precondition is why: **no declared sweep exists on their settings cells.** They carry the third provenance label, `undeclared-protocol`, and the two labels turn on **one predicate read both ways**: a declared protocol exists, or none was declared — never neither, never both.

**Two provenance labels were added by [#56](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/56). Neither is a standing**, on the same reasoning: a provenance label says *what was available*, a standing says *what the figure may be quoted as*, and conflating them is how the closed vocabulary above came to have a fifth value it should never have had.

- **`external-operands`** — **every figure in this file carries it, except where its settings say otherwise.** The corpus is untracked by decision (redistribution rights) and the estimator is out-of-repo, so every figure here rests on two things this repository does not contain. **Declared once rather than written on 90 rows**, because the condition is uniform — the same nine files and the same estimator throughout — and a label repeated 90 times is the "nine bare triples look like a table and are not one" failure this file already records against itself. It does **not** change any standing: the corpus and the estimator are **operands**, not axes, because they are not varied over. A reader holding the corpus can confirm they have these files from the **file digests** below.
- **`unlocated-estimator`** — marks a figure whose **estimator cannot be found at all**, which is strictly worse than external-and-located. **Membership is the token and the count is read off the rows, not written here** — the rule [#62](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/62) established for `undeclared-protocol` below, extended to this label by [#78](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/78). The rows are those under [#50](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/50) and [#63](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/63), and the coverage limit under [#55](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/55). **This count was wrong three times over** — it read `12` here, `10` in [#55](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/55)'s section, and the rows carried it on `9` — and [#63](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/63) is what last read it. **It now reads nine**, and the ninth is a malformed row; the known-limits entry carries that, and it is not adjudicated here. External-but-located is the ordinary condition of a research harness and earns no label of its own; `remeasure.py` is located by absolute path in `docs/research/residual-field-remeasurement.md`.

- **`undeclared-protocol`** — added by [#62](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/62). Marks a figure whose **settings cell names the instrument axis and declares no protocol version**. That is a **conjunction of two things this file already defines** and not a condition of its own: the instrument is one of the **seven axes**, and a figure must carry **all six** of its sub-values to be anything but `unpinned`. So the label says what was available — *nothing was declared* — and, on [#56](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/56)'s reasoning again, it changes no standing. **Membership is the token, so the count is derived by counting it and is not written here.** That is the whole point of the label: this file had **three** ways of writing the same condition — the phrase `no declared protocol`, an `as above` pointing at such a row, and a cell naming the instrument with only a draw — and the first two were one literal search apart while the third no phrasing change would ever have surfaced. A **fourth** phrasing is therefore undetectable, which is [#38](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/38)'s procedural enforcement arriving at a condition where a **declared token** does survive being typed differently: the token is what the count reads, not prose. See the *Undeclared protocols* section.

`legacy-sweep` **was, until [#59](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/59), defined but applied to no row** — a declared provision rather than a working one, and the register said so of itself. **It is now applied to the rows named in the [declared sweep](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/33) section by content**, and both keep their standing: their settings were not carried, which is `unpinned` on its own terms and [#47](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/47)'s ruling on them, while the label carries the fact their standing no longer states — that a sweep exists and its per-setting record was not kept. These two labels are the first this file actually uses, so they establish the convention rather than copy it; the third, `undeclared-protocol`, is written to them and to nothing else.

**No sentence in this file asserts a current row count.** Decided by [#78](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/78): the number of rows carrying a token is **derived by counting the token**, and a count written in prose is a claim this file has now made wrongly four times over — `unlocated-estimator` three times, the exception clause once — each time **written from a plan rather than from the tables**. Deleting the count is the fix rather than verifying it, because a derived count cannot drift and a maintained one only can. **A count recorded as a past state is not affected, and several are kept below**: the known-limits section records what each count used to read, and that is a record rather than a claim. **This is a declaration and not a mechanism**, the same shape as an `_Avoid_` line, and it makes a violation visible to review rather than to a tool — because no search can find a count written `twelve`.

### The two properties that make this a rule and not a caveat

1. **The spread never decides anything.** It *is* the figure's interval. There is no threshold anywhere in this file, so there is no number here that a judgement about acceptability can move.
2. **The rule binds the ticket that declares it.** [The background model](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/39) declared every residual-field-dependent figure stale and then reused one unmarked in the same comment. That is the shape every future violation will take if the declarer is exempt. It is not exempt.

### Exempt

Not corpus-derived measurements, and so not bound by the rule. Recorded here so the exemption is **visible rather than assumed**:

- **Definitional** — bounds and identities proved from a definition (`ceil(L·W/2)` as the independence number of the connectivity graph).
- **Synthetic** — results over constructed grids, probes or fixtures. A manufactured field says nothing about a real export.
- **Exact-oracle derived** — computations from rational arithmetic or an exact-integer oracle, independent of any corpus (`C₀ = 4/5`, `c₁` by `n mod 4`, the median-atom lemma's 2,011-case verification).
- **Raw-column** — statistics read off a column with no processing convention applied (value integrality, index origin, a raw median).

### Operand identity

The fingerprint above says what the figures were computed **in**. It says nothing about **what they were computed from**, and `CONTEXT.md`'s **Environment fingerprint** is explicit that it is not the place for that. This section is the place, added by [#56](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/56).

**A `file digest` is SHA-256 over a whole file's raw bytes, with no normalisation.** It answers exactly one question: *is this the file the figure was computed from?* It is **not** a claim that anyone can reproduce anything — the files it names are **not in this repository**, so a digest over one is traceable and not obtainable. That is deliberate, and it is why the digest exists rather than a stronger claim: **OKM can revise their educational samples, and if they do, every figure below silently changes provenance.** This is a **drift detector**, to be checked whenever the corpus is next obtained. It is not a reproducibility certificate, and this file's own rule — *a traceable-sounding figure that nobody can reproduce is worse than an absent one* — is honoured by saying so rather than by claiming more.

This is **not** the project's other hash. [#35](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/35) decided a **canonical payload hash** which is **byte-blind by design**: over parsed `binary64` responses, blind to padding, to declared lattice dimensions, and to every operator-typed value, so that an operator editing a note cannot re-frame an identical survey. **That hash cannot identify a file, by design.** A file digest cannot survive that edit. Two digests, opposite jobs, and the glossary states the contrast so that neither is assumed from the other.

### `scale_status` census — taken under [The not-emitted reason for an unusable robust scale](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/37)

Same residual field as the census above, so the same background, lattice and fingerprint. Two additions of its own: the **quantile convention** was computed in the pinned floor form (`j = floor(np)`, `gamma = 1/2` at `g = 0` else `1`, no endpoint clamping) rather than through a library, and the **scale estimator** is the pinned pair on one σ target under **`scale-agreement-v1`** — `σ(MAD) = MAD / Z₇₅` and `σ(IQR) = IQR / (2·Z₇₅)` with `Z₇₅ = Φ⁻¹(0.75) = 0.6744897501960817`, the disagreement magnitude normalised by `max(σ(MAD), σ(IQR))`, and the tolerance `u·(C₀ + c₁·L/s)/(1 − K·u)` with `C₀ = 4` odd / `5` even, `c₁ = 0 / 1/Z₇₅ / 2/Z₇₅` by `n mod 4`, `K = 11`, `u = 2⁻⁵³`, `L = max(|m̃|, |Q₂₅|, |Q₇₅|)`, `s = max(a, b)`.

**The disagreement quantity of record, and the two that are not.** Three normalisations of the same two estimates were in circulation and are now separated by name. The contract's `scale_status` comparison names **the relative magnitude `d = |a − b| / max(a, b)`** — dimensionless, in `[0,1]` identically, and exactly invariant to the calibration constant because numerator and denominator share it. That bound is load-bearing rather than convenient: `tie-tolerance-saturated` is the derivation that `tol ≥ 1` is exceeded by every possible pair, so it is decidable only against a quantity that cannot exceed `1`. **The tolerance is dimensionless**, so the comparison admits no other candidate.

| quantity | formula | status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `d` | `abs(a − b) / max(a, b)` | **of record** — the quantity `scale_status` compares against `tol` |
| `disagreement ÷ σ(MAD)` | `abs(a − b) / a` | reporting column only. Dimensionless and exactly constant-invariant, so quotable, but it **privileges MAD as the reference** and is *not* the quantity a `tol ≥ 1` argument runs on |
| `abs(a − b)` | — | **struck.** In residual units, and a **Response** is not comparable across scans, so a cross-scan table of it compares quantities that do not share a scale; it is also the only one of the three that moves under the pinned constant. Its sole consumer was a clearance claim now restated against `d`. Both estimates are recorded on `scale_status`, so the difference remains recoverable in any reader's own units — what is not recoverable is a comparison the contract forbids |

The two relative forms **coincide exactly when `σ(MAD) ≥ σ(IQR)`** (3 of the 6) and differ where `σ(IQR)` is the larger, by the ratio of the two estimates: **at most `1.17×`**, on `Pipeline`. A reader comparing the two columns will see a discrepancy of that size and it is arithmetic, not error.

**What the declarations settled, and what the first run refuted.** Two consequences followed from the declarations rather than from a measurement, and each retired a worry that was carried as open. Both hold. A third did not, and its refutation is the more useful result:

- **The detection count still saturates at the cell count.** Independent continuous noise on the payload leaves the residual's values pairwise distinct just as it does under the residual anchor, so `N = n` per polarity and [#43](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/43)'s attainment holds under either protocol. **REFUTED by [#50](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/50): saturation holds on 9 of 9 under the residual anchor and 0 of 9 under the payload anchor.** The premise, not the conclusion, is what failed — see the correction below.
- **Payload-protocol mask-invariance is strictly rarer than residual-protocol.** The bound is **`(1 + Λ)·a`**, where `Λ` is the **sup-norm Lipschitz constant of the background operator** — so it is a property of the **background model, not of the draw**. For the pinned median ladder `Λ = 1` and the bound is **`2a`** — and [#58](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/58) strengthened that from a fact about this background to a fact about **the whole class**: any **order-preserving, shift-equivariant** operator has `Λ = 1` exactly, since shift-equivariance gives `‖B(x+c) − B(x)‖∞ = ‖c‖∞` (so `Λ ≥ 1`) and monotonicity gives `‖Bx − By‖∞ ≤ ‖x − y‖∞` (so `Λ ≤ 1`). So `2a` is **forced**, not tuned, and neither a tighter nor a looser constant follows from any such background. The derivation, supplied by [#55](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/55) where this entry previously asserted it in a single clause: a draw perturbs **every** payload cell at once, so **per-input Lipschitz is the wrong property** — it would license `w·a` for a window of `w` cells. What is needed is that a median is **monotone and shift-equivariant**, hence **1-Lipschitz in the sup norm**: if every input moves by at most `a` then `x − a ≤ x′ ≤ x + a` pointwise, so by monotonicity the median moves by at most `a`. Each of the three window medians therefore moves by at most `a`, their **median across windows 3/5/9** moves by at most `a`, and `r_j = x_j − B_j` moves by at most `|Δx_j| + |ΔB_j| ≤ 2a`. **The bound is field-wide and is not attributable to any single perturbed cell** — it is attained when one cell moves by `+a` while its window median moves by `−a`, which is exactly why it cannot be tightened by inspecting one cell at a time. **No detrending stage enters `background-model-v1`**: the residual is `values − bg`, with `bg` the median across three window medians of the **raw** values, so no operator norm beyond `Λ = 1` contributes and **no re-derivation is required** — the conditional this ticket flagged is settled against itself. **Corrected and strengthened by [#58](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/58): the resolution is not that this model happens to omit a detrending stage, but that a stage of that kind is the only thing that could ever raise `Λ`.** An explicit trend term is a fitted operator that is **not** shift-equivariant — its slope term responds to a uniform offset — so a background bump can move `Λ` only by ceasing to be order-preserving and shift-equivariant. Since `Λ ≥ 1`, such a bump can only make payload-anchored invariance **rarer**, never more common, which is what makes the registry exclusion one-directional. Every threshold distance must clear twice the amplitude, so the guard reads `not-guaranteed` more often. **The derivation stands; the comparison is not observable here.** The guard reads `not-guaranteed` on **18 of 18** under **each** anchor, so there is no room to be rarer — and the two readings now live in **two rows** of their own, the residual-anchor one **`recorded`** and the payload-anchor one **`unpinned`**; they are never pooled. Under a `lattice-preserving` draw the payload perturbation is a lattice multiple bounded by `q·g`, so the residual bound is **`2·q·g`**; under the **continuous** draw `q·g = a` and it collapses to `2a`, which is why this bound never needed restating before now.

That asymmetry is **deliberate and stated here so it is not "repaired"**: deleting the three would make a figure unable to say what it answers, and adding them to the record would put a property of the instrument into the contract.

**The value lattice, and when `lattice-preserving` applies at all.** The quantum `g` is the largest value every observed response is an exact multiple of, computed on the printed decimals — a **per-scan fact carried in scan context**, not an axis, and never the declared precision. A draw of amplitude `a` quantises to a realised `±q·g` at `q = round(a/g)`, so **a scan where `q = 0` is not applicable**: the trial perturbs nothing, no figure is quoted from it, and the count of applicable scans travels with every figure taken under this draw. **Expect that count to be small** — a 1% amplitude is below one quantum wherever the residual population sd is at most `50·g`, which on an integral export means it bites only on the widest fields. An export whose responses are all identical has no lattice at all, and is likewise not applicable.

**Three rules [#55](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/55) added here, all of them about the realised perturbation rather than the requested one.**

1. **The amplitude that enters an invariance or sensitivity statement is the realised bound `q·g`, never `0.01·sd`.** The declared magnitude is a *request*; what the trial actually applied is `q·g`, and on this corpus that runs from **`0`** to **`777·g`** across the nine. A statement quoted at `0.01·sd` is therefore either exact by luck — as it is under the continuous draw, where `q·g = a` identically — or wrong by three orders of magnitude. Under `payload-perturbation-v1` the residual-level bound is derived through the background model as **`2·q·g`**; see the derivation above.
2. **`q = 0` makes a sensitivity result *vacuous*, not *stable*.** Nothing was perturbed, so nothing was tested. This is a distinct outcome from a measured stability and must never be recorded as one; it is the sharpest form of the rule that a figure names the question it answers, because the question here was not asked at all. The count of **applicable** scans is what travels with such a figure, and on this corpus it is **2 of 9**.
3. **The realised perturbation is bounded by `q·g`, and that bound is a property, not a hope.** Since `round` is monotone and odd-symmetric, `|round(eps/g)| ≤ round(|eps|/g) ≤ round(a/g) = q` for every draw with `|eps| ≤ a`, so **every perturbed value lies on the export's value lattice and `|perturbation| ≤ q·g`** — with `q = 0` forcing `round(eps/g) = 0` for every `|eps| ≤ a < g/2`, which is the identity case and is **derivable without a run**. These four checks, plus a coverage run confirming the quantisation branch executes at all, are the preconditions [#55](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/55) sets before any figure may be taken under this draw.

**The rounding rule is stated here because it was unstated and is not load-bearing.** `q = round(a/g)` is Python's builtin `round()` on a float with no `ndigits`, so it is **half-to-even**: `q = 0` exactly when `a/g ≤ 0.5`, and `q ≥ 1` exactly when `a/g > 0.5`. Half-to-even and half-up agree on **9 of 9** here — the nearest approach to a boundary is `2.1e-2`, on `Iron Box` — so the mode changes no recorded figure. It is recorded because this file already carries a **different** convention, `round_half_up`, for the metric echo, and two coexisting conventions with neither named is how the `>=`/`>` slip at the applicability boundary went unnoticed.

### [The definitional bound on the detection count](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/43) — three checks, kept separate

[#34](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/34) requires bound validity, witness attainment and production agreement to be **three** obligations rather than one, so they are three entries. Only the third is a measurement; the first two are definitional, and neither is established by the third.

| check | statement | basis | standing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **validity** | per polarity, **`c₁ ≤ N ≤ n`**, where `N` is the detection count, `n` the cells entering the hierarchy for that polarity, and `c₁` the components of that polarity's cell set. The summed count satisfies it too. | **Definitional, by charging.** Order levels by ascending magnitude, `g_j` the tie group at level `j`, so `Σ g_j = n`. Build active sets from the top magnitude down, adding one tie group at a time. The top level contributes at most `g_m` births. At each later step every component of the new active set either contains a cell of the group just added — new, hence a birth, at most `g_j` of them — or contains none, hence identical as a set to a component before, hence a continuation. So `N ≤ g_m + Σ_{j<m} g_j = n`. **Connectivity and `m` are never used.** | not a measurement |
| **attainment** | both ends reachable, and the **upper end is `n`**. | **Constructed, never read off the corpus.** Give every cell a **distinct magnitude**: each level then adds exactly one cell and yields exactly one birth, so `N = 1 + (n − 1) = n`, for any region under either connectivity — held in **2989 of 2989** randomised regions, and a theorem rather than a delicate arrangement. The lower end: with a single magnitude no component ever changes, so `N = c₁`. `N = n` is likewise attainable at **every** `(n, m)` including `m = 1` (scatter `n` mutually non-adjacent cells), so **no bound over `(n, m)` is tighter than `n`** and the bound is stated over `n` alone. | not a measurement |

**Three things this table is not evidence for.** The corpus **attains** the bound on `Anomaly at the Edge` positive at `N = n = 111`, and per [#30](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/30) that is **corroboration, not the witness** — the witness is the construction, and the bound is argued from it. **Saturation is attainment.** [#42](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/42)'s per-trial multiset being the constant measured-cell count on 9 of 9 is this bound being reached: perturbing the residual destroys the median atom, every measured cell enters, and near-surely distinct magnitudes give `N = n` per polarity. The node count cannot drift, because the ceiling is the cell count and the ceiling is reachable. **Its admissibility therefore rides on [#45](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/45)**: under a payload-perturbation protocol the atom may survive and this corpus may never attain the bound at all.

**Refuted, not superseded: the `2n − 1` bound.** [#40](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/40)'s premise was a **merge-tree** bound, and this is not a merge tree — the active set shrinks as the level rises, so components **split**, and the nodes are merged cell sets forming a laminar family rather than a tree. `2n − 1` is attained **only at `n = 1`**. `n + m − 1` is valid and weaker than `n`, and is dropped rather than stated alongside; its refutation was already void per [#44](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/44).

**Also retired here: the 2.3% connectivity sensitivity.** [#40](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/40) and [#43](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/43) both leaned on *"changing node counts by at most 2.3% between 4- and 8-connectivity"*. **Corrected by [#47](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/47): this paragraph was stale twice over.** It said the figure "appears in **no** register entry" and that "the 8-connectivity counts behind it are recorded **nowhere** for the node count" — but the counts **are** now recorded, as the `recorded` detection-count 4-versus-8 row above carrying all 36 values and the worst relative difference `0.27419`, with `N(8) ≤ N(4)` on 18 of 18. And it named a standing, `unpinned`, for a figure that **has no row**: a retired figure with no entry cannot have a standing, and `unrecoverable` — *retired, supports nothing* — is the correct one for it. It was never load-bearing: [#43](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/43)'s charging argument never invoked connectivity, so the connectivity question is closed on the proof instead.

## Known limits of this file

Stated here rather than discovered later.

- **Enforcement is procedural over figures, and mechanical over tokens.** A lint cannot check a **figure**: a literal search for `53 -> 167-186` misses the body of [Magnitude-instability](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/32) entirely, because it writes `→` and en-dashes, and that class of miss is not fixable by trying harder. Nor can the existing quote sites be retrofitted, because most are immutable comments. What *can* be checked by reading: this file is the only place a figure's standing is written, and the specification cites entries here rather than restating figures. **[#78](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/78) split the two and left the first standing**: a standing and a provenance label are **tokens in a known column**, so a check may read them, and the class of miss above cannot move a token between columns. **What no check can read is a count written in prose**, and the answer to that is deletion rather than verification.
- **A gate that can be evaded by typing an en-dash is worse than an honest rule**, because it reads as enforcement while enforcing nothing. **That is why there is no gate over a figure's value, and it is not why there is one over this file's tokens** - the two answer different questions, and the second could not be asked until a closed tokenised vocabulary existed to read. The owed check is **one test, several assertions, a hard gate, and no suppression**: a suppression comment is the escape hatch that turns a gate into the thing this bullet names. It lands **after** the ninth `unlocated-estimator` row is adjudicated, so the suite is never red on arrival. **Two holes are admitted rather than papered over**: nothing stops a prose count being reintroduced, and nothing can decide whether a settings cell is self-contained, because `as above` and a bare line reference are invisible to any search.
- **Unmarked figures in immutable comments cannot be repaired**, only superseded. That is why the withdrawn tuple is quoted in this file with its standing attached: a reader who reaches the old comment finds the number, and a reader who reaches this file finds out not to use it. The first reader is not protected, and no mechanism available here can protect them.
- **The residual-field subset is now here.** It was tracked in [Which residual-field figures survive re-measurement](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/42) and that ticket has closed, so the figures live in this file. One consequence is worth stating plainly: closing it produced a **protocol of record and a level rule of record** that did not exist before, and both are declared in the re-measurement section. Every later stability figure cites one of them rather than choosing its own.
- **A reproduction is not always a reproduction.** Re-measuring under `background-model-v1` reproduced the residual-field and scale censuses exactly, and reproduced the hierarchy's **population** and **level-composition** columns exactly too — but **not** the hierarchy's node count, which sits in the same tables as the columns that do reproduce. So a table can be seven columns traceable and one not, and "the same ticket measured it" is not a construction. The node-count entry above is left `unpinned` rather than replaced by a number of my own, because three readings of "node" give three numbers and I cannot show which produced the recorded one.
- **A per-export figure must be labelled by export, and this file got that wrong first.** The re-measurement's three per-export columns were written as bare sequences in the order the run produced them, which was **not** sorted-filename order — so the figures were unattributable to an export until re-derived, and one sequence was ordered differently from its neighbour. Nine bare triples look like a table and are not one. It is now labelled, and the general rule is the obvious one that was nonetheless missed: **a figure about "the corpus" is nine figures**, and a sequence is not a table.
- **Two readings of one concept, both measured, one wrong.** The level-composition columns reproduce **only** when a level is read as a **(polarity, magnitude)** pair and simultaneous entry is counted within one polarity. Grouped across polarities, the same computation reproduces **none** of the three columns. That is not a tuning knob but a definitional fact, and it was settled by the glossary correction rather than by the numbers — the numbers only ever had one answer. Recorded because the wrong reading produced a confident mismatch, and a confident mismatch is indistinguishable from a real defect until the definition is pinned.
- **One column was read wrongly before it was read right, and the wrong reading is the instructive one.** `Royal Tomb`'s largest per-level component count was first read as a component *size* and appeared to breach a provable upper bound on four of nine exports. It is a *count*, and specifically a **cross-polarity total** — which is a different defect, not the absence of one. Recorded because the failure mode is the general one: a corpus column whose name admits two readings can be made to look refuted, and a confident refutation is worse than an unmatched figure.
- **Every residual-field figure in this file rests on two things that are not in this repository.** The corpus is **untracked by decision**, pending redistribution rights, and the estimator is **out of repo**. [#56](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/56) found these are **not one condition but two, and one is worse than this paragraph previously stated**: `remeasure.py` is **located**, by absolute path in `docs/research/residual-field-remeasurement.md`, and every figure from [#39](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/39) onward depends on it — but `measure50.py` and `measure53.py`, which produced [#50](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/50)'s nine figures and the coverage figures under [#55](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/55), are named **nowhere in the tracked tree**. Those ten rows carry the `unlocated-estimator` label: a reader cannot even find their code. So these figures are **reproducible by the owner under the recorded fingerprint and by nobody else** — deterministic, reproduced bit-for-bit, and not regenerable from this repository alone. [#55](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/55) surfaced this while registering one missing column and **declined to treat it as that column's defect, because it is not**: the exposure is **register-wide**, and a per-entry label would have concealed it rather than recorded it. The known-limitation form of the condition is the provenance label **`external-operands`**, declared once above; **it is deliberately not a standing**, because it says what was *available* and a standing says what a figure may be *quoted as*. #55 named it as a fifth standing and that is withdrawn — the vocabulary is **closed**, and the same ticket made the mistake twice, also recording an **`argued`** standing that was never one of the four. **Partly closed by [#56](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/56): the operands are now digested**, under Operand identity above. What a digest buys is **drift detection, not reproduction** — the files are not in this repository, so a digest over one is traceable and not obtainable, and this is said rather than glossed. Three items are **owed to the specification handoff** and are recorded so they are not lost: **(1)** the **canonical payload hash** has no value, because [#35](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/35) is decided but implemented nowhere; **(2)** the ten `unlocated-estimator` rows need their scripts brought into the repository, since a digest of a file nobody can obtain identifies the code as little as it identifies the data; **(3)** `docs/research/residual-field-remeasurement.md`'s two unattributed sequences need attribution, which #55 deliberately did not perform. In-repo Python that computes a residual field is **absent**: `src/` holds a skeleton and the only such code in the tree is the one [#39](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/39) **rejected**.
- **Three registered figures were produced against a library median, and the register has no rule for the condition they share.** [#63](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/63) found that `measure50b.py` computed the median-atom fraction and the exactly-zero count against **`np.median`** rather than the pinned **`quantile-v1`**. The two agree at `p = 0.5` in value and differ only in the last place of the rounding — `(a+b)/2` against `0.5a + 0.5b` — so the exposure is **one unit in the last place of a median, on the seven of nine exports with an even measured-cell count**. Measured: **every affected figure is unchanged**, because the atom fraction is a *count* of cells exactly equal to the median and no cell on this corpus sits at a midpoint. **The sweep is now done, and it found three more sites in three more scripts.** [#64](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/64) read all sixteen leaves: **four call a library median, at eight sites**, and the second registered figure among them is **the top-8 lifetime movement spread, which no ticket had recorded as off-convention.** The hub is clean, and the confirmation is stronger than absence — `remeasure.py` routes every order statistic through `median_of` → `q2`, and its one library quantile is `Z75`, which is **a pinned literal asserted equal to `norm.ppf(0.75)`** rather than delegated to it. All seventeen file digests match Operand identity above, so the sweep read the recorded operands rather than whatever is on disk today. **No registered figure moves**, and the three reasons are different from one another, which is the finding: an **exact-equality count** moves only if a cell sits at the midpoint of its own two central order statistics, and none does on this corpus; a **median reported as a value** cannot move at the precision this file quotes, since a one-ulp difference is `2⁻⁵³` relative against a quoted resolution near `10⁻⁷`; and a **cancellation residual** — `σ(MAD)·Z75 - MAD`, quoted at `2.22e-16` — **would** be moved by it, and is safe only because the hub computes it on the pinned convention, not because of anything about the quantity. **What remains open is what the register does with the condition**, which it has no rule for and no row for: [that question is now a ticket](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/108). **Two further findings came out of the same reading, and neither is a one-ulp exposure.** The **nine-figure atom column** in the `lattice-preserving` coverage row below is computed by `measure53.py` against `np.median` and is **credited to no script at all** in the attribution table above, while `verify55.py` computes the same quantity pinned through `robust_scale.atom_fraction` and this file does not say which of the two produced the row — and [#53](https://github.com/abdoupk/groundscan-analyzer-v2/issues/53)'s three attributed figures are median-free by inspection.
