# 0001 Presentation consumers live in sibling packages, not in the engine

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-10
- **Tracker:** #1 (map), #19 (dependency verdicts), #115 (CLI scope), #145 (HTML record), #146 (terminal view)
- **Affects:** `packages/*`, `src/groundscan_analyzer/`

## Context

Three closed tickets exclude the same thing from the engine.

#1 lists under its fog: "Reporting, report generation, viewer, visualisation, plotting, TUI.
Explicitly excluded from v2 scope." In the same breath it permits: "those may later exist as
external consumers of the engine's structured results; the engine must not be shaped to serve
them."

#115 puts terminal interfaces out of scope as *products*, restating the same permissive clause.

#19 evaluated the dependency set and ruled `rich` and `typer` out — not on cost grounds but
because `rich`'s entire value proposition is presentational, and presentational weight is what v2
declines to want. It also recorded a corollary: "ergonomics never admits," so a package wanted for
operator convenience is a dev dependency or nothing. `textual` was never a candidate; it is
excluded a fortiori as a presentational toolkit.

Read narrowly, a terminal UI in this repository is a contradiction. Read as the map actually
writes it, the exclusion is scoped: **no reporting, viewer, visualisation, plotting, or TUI in the
engine**, and the permissive second clause names exactly where such a thing may live.

`packages/groundscan-record` (#145) is the first thing built on that reading — a separate
distribution that depends on the engine as a library and renders one Document to one
self-contained HTML file. It carries `jinja2`, a dependency the engine does not have and may not
acquire. It changed no engine module.

`packages/groundscan-tui` (#146) is the second: an interactive terminal view of one export,
carrying `textual`.

The point of this record is that the reading is a decision, not a default. Left unrecorded, the
next agent re-derives it from scratch, and the two plausible answers — "the map forbids it" and
"the map permits it" — lead to opposite implementations.

## Decision

A presentation consumer of the engine's structured results lives in its own package under
`packages/`, never in `src/groundscan_analyzer/`.

Concretely, and binding on every such package:

1. **The engine is not shaped to serve the consumer.** No module, field, type, or public function
   is added, renamed, or relaxed for a consumer's benefit. Where a consumer needs something the
   engine keeps private — the `--perturbation` and `--displacement` parsers are the live case — the
   consumer reads through the shipped CLI surface rather than the engine opening up. Duplicating
   a private parser inside the consumer is worse than both: it creates a second parser for one
   contract, and it drifts silently.
2. **The consumer's dependencies are its own.** `textual` and `jinja2` are declared in the
   package's `pyproject.toml` and nowhere else. The engine's `dependencies` set is governed by
   #19 and does not move.
3. **The consumer renders what the engine emits.** Where the record already carries the fact, the
   consumer shows the fact. Where the fact is not recoverable — the background field is not; only
   its five constants reach the record — the consumer does not reconstruct it and does not
   present a substitute.
4. **The consumer adds no verdict.** #19's corollary and `CONTEXT.md`'s `Field ready` agree: a
   consumer may compute readiness, and the engine's record must never be read as asserting one.
   `packages/groundscan-record` declined to compute one; `packages/groundscan-tui` declines too.
   A fold over named fields is admissible only where the formula and its inputs are on screen
   beside the result.
5. **The engine's closed vocabulary reaches the consumer in one direction.** A consumer inherits
   `groundscan_analyzer.vocabulary`'s declared ban lists and lints its own shipped labels against
   them. The engine does not walk into `packages/` to check: that would couple the engine's gate
   to a package that may not exist.
6. **The consumer is gated as hard as the engine.** Its mypy, pytest, coverage and deptry gates
   run on the commit path, not on demand.

## Consequences

- `packages/*` is now a real, governed surface rather than a dumping ground. `groundscan-record`
  shipped with only ruff on the commit path; its typecheck and coverage never ran. #146 closes
  that for both.
- #1's fog list stays true as written. Nothing was added to the engine, so nothing contradicts it.
- A future consumer — a plotter, a web viewer, a report generator — has a settled pattern to
  follow and does not reopen this question.
- The cost is real and accepted: two more mypy runs and two more pytest runs per commit, and a
  maintained `uv.lock` and `audit-requirements.txt` that must be regenerated by their recorded
  commands rather than hand-edited.

> _Supersedes nothing. #1, #19 and #115 remain exactly as written; this record fixes how their
> permissive clause is read, not what they say._
